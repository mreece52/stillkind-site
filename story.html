The Story

Still Kind was built behind a bar.

Two decades of bartending across both the east and west coast, and now firmly planted in the beautiful city of Chicago, taught me something most leadership books miss: the best thing you bring into a room isn't a system, it's yourself, and you get to decide what that self brings.

There is so much good in this world that does not get the recognition or credit it deserves. This company was designed with that in mind. A reminder that we have no idea what anyone is going through at any given time, and we have a small window to control the vibe, set the tone, change the outlook of a day.

A summer very recently, I had to put my dog down. That same week, I was working one of the busiest bars in the city, the kind of place that gets slammed all summer because it sits right on the water. There was no slowing down, no room to step away.

It mattered to me, more than almost anything, to still be kind through that, even while I was devastated. That reciprocation of kindness was just as important to me, even with no one around me knowing what had happened, because I never wanted anyone to carry my pain for me.

The ability to still be kind sets the vibe. It lets the room breathe, even when I'm the one struggling to. Still Kind exists because that skill (presence, connection, consistency) can be taught. Not just to bartenders. To any leader willing to learn the part everyone else skips.
